Run four project gates

  1. Outcome gate: name the client decision or operational result, not only the consultant activity.
  2. Scope gate: list deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, owners, milestones, and acceptance evidence.
  3. Change gate: require a written impact record for added work, timing, fees, and client inputs.
  4. Closeout gate: reconcile approvals, invoices, access removal, files, methods, rights proposals, and unresolved terms.

Project evidence to collect

Before work starts

Approved brief, stakeholder map, current-state data, system-access owner, deliverable inventory, assumptions, exclusions, baseline dates, and budget input.

At every milestone

Dated work product, source data, decision log, consolidated feedback, acceptance test, approver, change requests, dependency delays, and invoice support.

At closeout

Final approval, output register, method and third-party material register, access revocation, deletion confirmation, handoff inventory, open risks, and final accounting.

A useful project record ties every deliverable to a source, owner, approval test, decision date, and unresolved question. Extra paragraphs cannot replace missing project evidence.

Worker status and intellectual property

Record facts before writing conclusions

For U.S. federal employment-tax purposes, the IRS says the actual relationship matters and groups relevant facts into behavioral control, financial control, and the type of relationship. A project record should capture those facts instead of deciding status from the heading or a checkbox.

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes ownership of a work from ownership of the copy that contains it. Identify consultant methods, client material, project outputs, third-party inputs, and any proposed transfer or license separately. A transfer of U.S. copyright ownership generally requires a signed writing by the rights owner.

Primary sources used for this checklist

  • IRS: Employee (common-law employee)
  • IRS Topic 762: Independent contractor vs. employee
  • U.S. Copyright Office: Chapter 2 - Ownership and Transfer

Project-based consulting agreement questions

What should a project-based consulting agreement define?
Record the business objective, named deliverables, exclusions, dependencies, milestones, acceptance evidence, change process, payment inputs, access needs, and unresolved legal terms. A project title alone does not define completion.
Does calling someone an independent contractor determine worker status?
No. In the United States, the actual working relationship and evidence of behavioral control, financial control, and the relationship of the parties matter. Record the facts and obtain qualified advice when classification is uncertain.
Does paying a consultant automatically transfer copyright?
Do not assume so. Identify pre-existing methods, client material, project outputs, third-party inputs, and the proposed transfer or license separately. Have the final language reviewed for the applicable jurisdiction.
How should consulting deliverables be accepted?
Name the approver, objective acceptance evidence, review window, correction process, and dated approval record for each milestone or final deliverable.
