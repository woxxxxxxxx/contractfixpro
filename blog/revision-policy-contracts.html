Revision Policy Clauses for Design, Writing, and Video Projects

Set practical revision limits without damaging client relationships.

By ContractFixPro Editorial TeamReviewed July 24, 20268 min read

Set practical revision limits without damaging client relationships.

Key takeaways

  • Define a round and who consolidates feedback.
  • Separate corrections from new creative direction.
  • Set feedback windows and deemed-approval rules carefully.

Revision rules clients can understand

A brand project includes two consolidated revision rounds. Each round requires one written feedback list within five business days. New concepts, direction changes, or requests after approval are quoted as additional work.

Run a contract-specific review

A revision policy should describe a decision loop, not merely a number. Name who consolidates feedback, when a round begins, how long the client has to respond, and what happens when feedback contradicts the approved brief or reopens an accepted stage.

1. Define a round

Treat one consolidated set of comments from the authorized reviewer as one round.

2. Set review windows

Give each party a response period and explain how silence affects the schedule.

3. Protect approved stages

Move reopened concepts or changed direction into a paid change process.

4. Price additional work

State the hourly rate or fixed quote process before extra revisions begin.

Evidence to retain

Keep timestamped drafts, consolidated comments, approval messages, reopened-stage requests, estimates, and final acceptance.

Worked example

Three stakeholders sending separate comments should not silently become three rounds. The client project owner can combine them into one prioritized response before the next revision starts.

Warning signs

  • Unlimited revisions remove the project boundary.
  • Feedback arrives from multiple uncoordinated reviewers.
  • A revision clause ignores schedule impact.
