Frequently Asked Questions

How many revision rounds should a video editing contract include?

There is no universal number. Choose a limit that matches the price and schedule, then define one round as one consolidated list of changes. State that a new concept, replacement footage, or changes after approval require a written scope and fee update.

Who provides the music and sound effects?

Record who supplies each asset and who pays for licenses. The agreement should require the client to confirm rights for client-supplied footage, music, logos, fonts, and artwork, while the editor should obtain written approval before buying third-party assets.

How should raw footage and project files be handled?

Treat exports, editable project files, and raw footage as separate deliverables. State whether Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or After Effects files are included, and write an exact deletion date or retention period instead of assuming indefinite storage.

What a Video Editing Contract Should Decide

A useful video editor contract is more than a project name and price. It should turn the handoff, feedback, asset, and delivery decisions that commonly delay post-production into written terms both sides can verify. Use the generator above as a working draft, then compare it with the decisions below.

Scope and deliverables

  • Number, runtime, resolution, and aspect ratio of each cut
  • Captions, color grade, audio mix, motion graphics, thumbnails, and source cleanup
  • Which editable project files, proxies, and raw assets are included

Feedback and acceptance

  • Who can approve a cut and where consolidated feedback is submitted
  • How many revision rounds the fee includes
  • What counts as a new direction and how extra work is approved

Assets and rights

  • Who licenses music, stock footage, fonts, logos, and plug-ins
  • When ownership or a usage license transfers
  • Whether the editor may show the released work in a portfolio

Schedule and storage

  • Dates for first cut, feedback, final approval, and exports
  • How late footage or feedback changes the schedule
  • How long source footage and project files are retained

Example scope language to adapt

The editor will deliver one 6-8 minute 4K master, one 1080p MP4, and three 9:16 social cuts up to 30 seconds each. The fee includes color correction, dialogue cleanup, licensed client-supplied music, captions from the approved script, and two revision rounds. One round means one consolidated feedback document. Replacement footage, a new creative direction, or changes requested after final approval require a written change order.

Ownership Is Not the Same as File Delivery

Keep three questions separate: who owns the final edit, who may use third-party assets, and whether editable project files are delivered. A client can receive broad rights to the final exported video without automatically receiving the editor's reusable templates, plug-ins, working files, or licensed stock assets. Write the intended arrangement instead of relying on the word “ownership.”

In the United States, a commissioned work is not automatically a “work made for hire” in every situation. The U.S. Copyright Office explains the statutory tests and the importance of a signed writing. For a valuable or unusual project, have a qualified lawyer review the final language for the applicable jurisdiction.

Primary references

  • U.S. Copyright Office Circular 30: Works Made for Hire
  • U.S. Copyright Act, Chapter 2: Copyright Ownership and Transfer
