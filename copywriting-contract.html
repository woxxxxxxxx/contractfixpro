Use the planner in four passes

  1. Freeze the brief: audience, offer, channel, deliverable list, exclusions, voice, source materials, and required reviewers.
  2. Build a claim sheet for every objective statement, statistic, testimonial, certification, comparison, and regulated-product assertion.
  3. Define revision rounds, new-direction triggers, acceptance evidence, file versions, copyright path, attribution, portfolio use, and AI workflow.
  4. Generate the planning draft, replace placeholders, verify it with both parties, and obtain legal or specialist review where the subject or jurisdiction requires it.

Copywriting contract review boundaries

This planner creates an operational record, not legal advice or a signed-ready agreement. The final language depends on the parties, worker relationship, location, subject matter, advertising channel, regulated-product rules, confidentiality needs, privacy obligations, evidence behind claims, and the licenses or terms attached to client and third-party material.

Copyright ownership should be explicit rather than assumed. U.S. copyright law distinguishes ownership of a copy from ownership of copyright, and a transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a signed writing. Identify final deliverables, drafts, concepts, research, pre-existing material, third-party content, byline, portfolio rights, and the timing and scope of any assignment or license for qualified review.

Advertising copy also needs a claim-evidence workflow. The FTC states that advertising claims must be truthful, not deceptive or unfair, and evidence-based; its small-business guidance says advertisers need a reasonable basis for objective claims before an ad runs. The client and qualified reviewers should approve claims, substantiation, qualifications, disclosures, testimonials, and regulated-product language before publication.

AI-assisted work needs an explicit production record. The U.S. Copyright Office's AI initiative addresses copyrightability of generative-AI outputs and the role of human contribution. Record approved tools, prohibited confidential inputs, human authorship and revision, fact checking, disclosure, retained records, tool terms, and ownership review rather than promising a result based only on tool use.

Copywriting contract planning questions

How should revisions be defined?

Define included rounds, the named approver, consolidated feedback format, response window, and the boundary between correcting work against the brief and changing the brief or direction.

What evidence should support advertising claims?

Create a claim sheet that identifies each objective statement, the source supplied or approved by the client, required qualifications or disclosures, reviewer, approval date, and where the evidence is stored.

Does payment automatically transfer copyright in copy?

Do not assume payment alone transfers copyright. Identify final deliverables, drafts, concepts, pre-existing materials, third-party content, transfer or license timing, and retained portfolio rights for qualified legal review.

How should AI-assisted writing be addressed?

Record whether tools are prohibited, permitted with approval, or subject to disclosure; also document confidential-input restrictions, fact checking, human revision, tool terms, record retention, and ownership review.

What counts as acceptance?

Use a written checklist tied to the approved brief, required formats, factual source sheet, link and spelling checks, brand and legal review, and a named acceptance record rather than relying on silence or publication by default.
