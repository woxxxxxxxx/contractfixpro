Run four production gates

  1. Brief gate: agree on audience, message, runtime, versions, references, exclusions, budget, and decision owner.
  2. Shoot-ready gate: confirm crew, equipment, locations, access, releases, licensed inputs, permits, safety, and backup plan.
  3. Post-production gate: identify edit stages, consolidated feedback, technical specs, captions, review rounds, and acceptance evidence.
  4. Release gate: verify permissions, final files, approved usage scope, credits, archive plan, and unresolved terms.

Decision evidence to collect

Before filming

Approved brief, shot list, schedule, location permission, participant status, call sheet, asset log, and insurance/permit requests.

During production

Camera and audio reports, slate/file map, incident notes, release exceptions, approved deviations, and redundant media status.

Before release

Licensed-asset register, dated review links, consolidated notes, final approval, delivery checksum, and archive/deletion record.

Approved storyboard, two location permissions, participant release tracker, stock/music license register, call sheets, and camera/audio reports.

Two consolidated review rounds, marketing director as approver, dated final link, checksum delivery log, and 60-day raw-media archive.

A useful record connects every deliverable and usage proposal to a source file, permission, approver, deadline, and unresolved question. Extra paragraphs cannot replace missing production evidence.

Rights and flight boundaries

Record facts before writing conclusions

The U.S. Copyright Office classifies motion pictures as audiovisual works and distinguishes copyright ownership from ownership of the physical or digital copy. A production record should therefore identify authorship inputs, client-supplied material, licenses, final exports, raw media, and any proposed transfer or license separately.

For U.S. commercial drone work, the FAA's Part 107 framework can require a certificated remote pilot and may require airspace authorization or a waiver depending on the planned operation. Do not promise a drone deliverable until the pilot, aircraft, location, operating conditions, approvals, insurance evidence, and backup shot are recorded.

Primary sources used for this checklist

  • U.S. Copyright Office: Motion Pictures and Audiovisual Works
  • Copyright Act, Chapter 2: Ownership and Transfer
  • FAA: Small UAS Regulations (Part 107)
  • FAA: Part 107 Airspace Authorizations

Video production agreement questions

Should raw footage and project files be listed separately from final videos?

Yes. Final exports, raw footage, project files, graphics, audio stems, and archive services are different deliverables. List each item, format, transfer method, due date, and retention period.

Does paying for a video automatically transfer copyright?

Do not assume so. Record the intended ownership or license model, the specific rights and uses involved, and the source of each input. Have the final language reviewed for the applicable facts and jurisdiction.

What should be recorded for a commercial drone shot in the United States?

Record the pilot, certificate status, aircraft, location, airspace authorization or waiver status when applicable, operating conditions, insurance evidence, and backup shot plan.

How do review rounds avoid scope disputes?

Name one approver, require consolidated timestamped notes, define each review stage, distinguish corrections from new creative direction, and record the written acceptance of the final version.
